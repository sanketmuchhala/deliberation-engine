# Deliberation Decision Document
**Feature Request:** The CRM should alert us when our relationship with a country goes cold.

**Termination Reason:** `CONVERGENCE_ALL_ISSUES_RESOLVED_OR_ESCALATED (CRITIC_SIGNALED_CONVERGENCE)` | **Final Disagreement Score:** `0.0` | **Confidence (Proposer / Critic):** `0.72` / `0.20`

## Executive Summary
v1 will flag a Country as cold when its most recent logged Engagement is older than a single global 90-day threshold. A nightly job writes system-filled fields on the existing Country entity, shows a read-only Cold badge and filter, and sends one in-app notification and one generic email to the assigned Regional Coordinator per transition, with the first post-launch run acting as a silent baseline. Suppression for active Missions or scheduled Engagements is capped at 180 days, and only records visible to every user who can view the Country are counted. The main trade-off argued was alert accuracy and relationship coverage versus simplicity and confidentiality: restricted records and sensitive email content were handled by narrowing what the job counts and what the email says, rather than by adding configuration or workflow. All six challenges ended ACCEPTED, no scope or success criteria are pending, and no issues were escalated, so no human decision is outstanding.

## 1. Agreed Scope
### Included in Scope (v1 MVP)
- [S1] Define 'cold' as a deterministic rule over existing Engagement records: a country is cold when its most recent logged Engagement (any Contact, any Project or Mission, any channel) is older than a single global threshold of 90 days. No new fields for Regional Coordinators to fill in.
- [S2] A nightly scheduled job evaluates every active Country against the S1 rule and writes derived, system-filled fields (last_engagement_date, is_cold, cold_since, cold_alerted_at) on the existing Country entity. No new tables and no manual input. The job counts only Engagements that are visible to every user who can view the Country under the existing access model, so Engagements restricted under that model never influence the derived values. A Country with no Engagement on record shows 'No engagement logged' and is never flagged cold or alerted.
- [S3] Suppress the cold flag while a Country has an active Mission, or a Mission/Project Engagement scheduled within the next 60 days, since the relationship is evidently being worked. Suppression has a hard cap: once the last logged Engagement is older than 180 days (2x threshold), the Country is flagged cold regardless of open Missions or scheduled Engagements. The rule reads existing Mission/Project data only, and counts only Missions and scheduled Engagements that are visible to every user who can view the Country under the existing access model, so restricted Missions or scheduled Engagements never suppress or release the flag.
- [S4] When a Country transitions from not-cold to cold, send one in-app notification and one email to the Regional Coordinator assigned to that Country. Fires once per transition, with no repeat reminders. The first nightly run after launch is a silent baseline: it writes flags and badges but sends no notifications, and alerts fire only for transitions seen after it. If the Country has no assigned coordinator or the coordinator's user is deactivated, the alert is not marked as sent (cold_alerted_at stays empty) and delivery is retried each night until an active coordinator exists. The in-app notification shows the country name, last engagement date and a record link. The email is generic and contains no country name or cold status ('You have a new CRM alert, sign in to view').
- [S5] Show a read-only 'Cold' badge and 'last engaged N days ago' on the existing Country record and in the existing Country list view, and make it filterable. The badge and alert content use the existing Country access permissions, so users who cannot view a Country cannot see its cold status or alerts.
- [S6] The flag clears automatically the next nightly run after any new Engagement is logged for the Country. No acknowledge, snooze or dismiss workflow.

### Pending Human Decision (relies on an escalated issue)
- None.

### Explicitly Excluded from Scope
- Per-country, per-contact, or per-relationship-tier configurable thresholds
- Relationship health scoring, sentiment analysis, or any ML/predictive model
- Snooze, acknowledge, assign, or escalation workflows and alert queues
- New dashboards, cold-country lists, or reports beyond the filter on the existing Country list view
- Alerts for individual contacts going cold (e.g., a ministry contact leaving) as opposed to the country as a whole
- Engagement quality weighting (e.g., a courtesy email versus a ministerial meeting)
- Filtering Engagements by type or by whether the Contact is still in post
- Automatic ingestion of engagement from email, calendar, or other external systems
- Adding new manual fields to be filled in by Regional Coordinators
- Fallback recipients, leave and out-of-office handling, or escalation chains for alerts
- Per-viewer recomputation of derived values; v1 uses one value computed from non-restricted Engagements and non-restricted Missions
- Defining which countries, engagements or missions are diplomatically sensitive or restricted, setting clearance rules, or deciding whether email may carry any CRM alert: these are policy and classification matters for humans, so v1 inherits existing access controls only
- Backfilling or cleaning historical engagement data

### Success Criteria
- The nightly job evaluates 100% of active Countries and completes within 15 minutes, with is_cold and last_engagement_date matching a manual spot-check of 20 sampled countries with zero discrepancies. (measures S1, S2, S3)
- The first post-launch nightly run sends zero notifications. After that, each not-cold to cold transition produces exactly one notification to an active assigned Regional Coordinator within 24 hours, with zero repeat alerts for the same cold episode, verified in the first 30 days post-launch. (measures S4)
- For Countries with a blank or deactivated coordinator in test, no alert is marked sent, and the alert is delivered on the first nightly run after an active coordinator is assigned, in 100% of test cases. (measures S4)
- Fewer than 10% of cold alerts in the first 60 days are reported by Regional Coordinators as false alarms (e.g., relationship was active but engagement was not logged), measured via a one-click 'not useful' link or a short feedback survey, used to decide whether to tune the threshold. (measures S1, S3, S4)
- A user without access to a given Country sees no cold badge, filter result, or notification for it, verified with a permission test covering at least 3 role types. (measures S4, S5)
- In a permission test, adding or removing an Engagement restricted under the existing access model changes no derived Country field (last_engagement_date, is_cold) visible to a user who lacks access to that Engagement, in 100% of test cases. (measures S2, S5)
- In a permission test, opening, closing, or scheduling a Mission or future Engagement that is restricted under the existing access model changes no derived Country field (is_cold, cold_since) and triggers no notification visible to a user who lacks access to it, in 100% of test cases. (measures S3, S5)
- A Country with an open Mission or a scheduled future Engagement and a last logged Engagement older than 180 days is flagged cold in 100% of test cases, and one with a last Engagement of 100 days or less is suppressed. (measures S3)
- Every alert email sent in testing contains no country name and no cold status, only the generic sign-in prompt, in 100% of sampled emails. (measures S4)
- After a new Engagement is logged for a cold Country, the cold flag clears by the next nightly run in 100% of test cases, with zero coordinator actions required. (measures S5, S6)

## 2. Surfaced Assumptions & Deliberation Outcomes
- **[A1] 'Cold' means time elapsed since the last logged Engagement with the country, i.e. an absence of recorded contact, and not a qualitative judgement of relationship quality or sentiment.** (Challenged: `True` -> Disposition: `ACCEPTED`)
  - *Notes:* C5 (departed or administrative contacts resetting the clock) was defended and ended ACCEPTED; cold stays defined as absence of recorded contact, with no filtering by Engagement type or contact status.
- **[A2] A single global 90-day threshold is an acceptable starting default for all ~100 countries, and the existing Engagement history is logged consistently enough for it to be meaningful.** (Challenged: `True` -> Disposition: `REVISED_AND_ACCEPTED`)
  - *Notes:* C1 (day-one alert storm) ended ACCEPTED after the first nightly run was made a silent baseline and Countries with no Engagement are never flagged. The single 90-day threshold stays, with a false-alarm criterion to guide later tuning.
- **[A3] Every Country already has a Regional Coordinator (or equivalent owner) in the CRM, and the existing Country/Mission access model governs who may view a Country and its data.** (Challenged: `True` -> Disposition: `REVISED_AND_ACCEPTED`)
  - *Notes:* C2 ended ACCEPTED after alerts were made to retry nightly until an active coordinator exists (cold_alerted_at stays empty), and C3 ended ACCEPTED after S2 and S3 were limited to Engagements, Missions and scheduled Engagements visible to everyone who can view the Country.
- **[A4] Active Missions and scheduled future Engagements indicate a live relationship, so a country with them should not be flagged cold even if no Engagement was recently logged.** (Challenged: `True` -> Disposition: `REVISED_AND_ACCEPTED`)
  - *Notes:* C4 (zombie Missions and placeholder Engagements) ended ACCEPTED after suppression was capped at 180 days since the last logged Engagement and limited to Engagements scheduled within 60 days.
- **[A5] The CRM already has in-app notification and email delivery capability that can be reused. If not, in-app notification and the badge alone are sufficient for v1.** (Challenged: `True` -> Disposition: `REVISED_AND_ACCEPTED`)
  - *Notes:* C6 (country name and cold status in email) ended ACCEPTED after the email was made generic with no country name or cold status. Whether email may carry any CRM alert remains a policy matter, with in-app and badge as the fallback.

| C1 | A2 | REVISED | 1 | `ACCEPTED` | The first nightly run after launch will flag every country whose engagement history is stale or was never logged consistently. They learn to ignore the alert, and the real cold country is missed. A single 90-day threshold also fires constantly for low-tempo relationships and never for high-tempo ones. | C2 | A3 | REVISED | 1 | `ACCEPTED` | The alert goes once, to the single assigned Regional Coordinator, with no repeats. Coordinators turn over often across 100 countries. If the coordinator has left, is on leave, or the assignment is blank or stale, the one-time alert goes to a deactivated mailbox or nobody. Because the alert fires only once per transition, the country then stays cold in silence and nobody is ever told. The assumption that every Country has a valid, current owner is untested, and the design has no fallback recipient or 'no owner' detection. |
| C3 | A3 | REVISED -> REVISED | 2 | `ACCEPTED` | The derived last_engagement_date and is_cold are computed by a nightly job over ALL Engagement records for a country, then shown to anyone who can view the Country. Under least-privilege, a staff member may see a Country but not a restricted Mission, Contact, or Engagement. A user who sees 'last engaged 2 days ago' on a country where they know of no recent activity learns that a confidential engagement occurred. The same applies to a country that silently flips from cold to warm. S5 says Country permissions apply, but it does not say the derived value is computed per viewer's visible engagements or that restricted engagements are excluded or masked. The system-filled field on the Country entity bypasses record-level restrictions. |
| C4 | A4 | REVISED | 1 | `ACCEPTED` | Suppression of the cold flag while a country has any 'active Mission' or any scheduled future Engagement creates false negatives. Missions are often left open long after real activity stops, and future engagements are often placeholders that get cancelled or never marked complete. A country with a zombie mission or a stale calendar entry is never flagged, even after a year of no real contact. This is exactly the relationship the feature should catch. When the mission is eventually closed, the country may flip to cold abruptly, with no engagement-age context. The proposal has no staleness bound on the suppressing mission or scheduled engagement, such as a cap on how long it can suppress. |
| C5 | A1 | DEFENDED | 1 | `ACCEPTED` | The rule counts 'any Contact, any channel' as warming the relationship. An engagement logged with a ministry contact who has since left office, with a mission delegate unrelated to the host government, or a bulk or administrative entry such as a mass circular or an internal note logged as an Engagement, resets the 90-day clock. The country shows 'not cold' even though there is no live channel to anyone currently in post. Conversely, a country engaged only through a single departed minister's staff looks warm. 'Absence of recorded contact' is not the same as a working relationship when contact turnover is the main real-world risk. There is no filter on engagement type or on whether the contact is still active. |
| C6 | A5 | REVISED | 1 | `ACCEPTED` | S4 sends email containing the country name and 'gone cold' status. Email leaves the CRM's access control and persists in inboxes, mobile previews, forwards, and backups. In a diplomatically sensitive setting, a message saying relationship with Country X is cold is itself sensitive. If the coordinator has lost access, or the mail goes to a personal or shared mailbox, it leaks. The proposal states in-app notification and email are both sent, and offers email as a given if it exists. There is no minimal-content email, such as a generic 'you have a new alert, sign in to view'. Reuse of an existing channel does not guarantee it is safe for this content. |
