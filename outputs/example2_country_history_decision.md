# Deliberation Decision Document
**Feature Request:** Project managers should be able to see the full history of engagement with a country before starting a new mission.

**Termination Reason:** `CONVERGENCE_ALL_ISSUES_RESOLVED_OR_ESCALATED (CRITIC_SIGNALED_CONVERGENCE)` | **Final Disagreement Score:** `0.0` | **Confidence (Proposer / Critic):** `0.78` / `0.45`

## Executive Summary
Version 1 adds a read-only Engagement History tab to the Country record and an informational summary on the Create Mission screen. Both show only the Engagements the user can already see, filtered per record by Mission/Project access. The tab lists existing Engagement records with 200-character summary truncation, basic filters, Contact status labels, and one access-log entry for each open. The main trade-off was exposure and data staleness against zero new data entry and no new schema. The Critic's five challenges (C1-C5) were each answered with cheap query, display, and logging changes and ended ACCEPTED in round 2. No scope is pending and no issue was escalated, so no human decision is currently required for this release.

## 1. Agreed Scope
### Included in Scope (v1 MVP)
- [S1] Read-only 'Engagement History' tab on the existing Country record, listing existing Engagement records for that country in reverse-chronological order (date, type, linked Contact, linked Project/Mission, summary text shown truncated to the first 200 characters with an expand-on-click control), with server-side pagination. Opening the tab writes one entry (user, country, timestamp) to the existing application access/audit log; no new table and no review screen.
- [S2] Access to the history tab inherits the existing Country/Mission permission model, applied per record: every Engagement row is filtered by the same Engagement-level Mission/Project access check used in existing Engagement views. An Engagement is never shown merely because the user can see its Country or a linked Contact. No new roles, flags, or per-country settings.
- [S3] Pre-mission entry point: on the 'Create Mission' screen, once a country is selected, show a link/inline summary (engagement count, date of most recent logged engagement, count of currently active missions) that opens the S1 history view. All figures are computed after the same per-record permission filter as S1 and labelled 'visible to you'. If the user lacks access to the selected country, show only the message 'No access to this country's history - request via your Regional Coordinator' with no counts and no link. Purely informational; it does not block or gate mission creation.
- [S4] Basic filters on the history view using existing fields only: date range, engagement type, and linked Project/Mission. No saved views and no free-text search beyond the existing summary field search, if already available.
- [S5] Every Engagement shown displays its linked Contact's name and role as currently stored, with a link to the Contact record and a fixed note that contact details are current, not as of the engagement date. Contacts marked inactive or departed are visibly labelled, and a blank or unknown status is labelled 'status unknown'. The Contact's existing system 'record last updated' date is shown. Existing fields only; no new data entry.

### Pending Human Decision (relies on an escalated issue)
- None.

### Explicitly Excluded from Scope
- Any new data entry by Regional Coordinators: no new required fields, tags, or 'mission relevance' flags on Engagements
- New entities or tables (no separate 'History' or 'Briefing' object); the view is a query over existing Country, Contact, Mission, Project, and Engagement records
- AI-generated summaries, scoring, or 'relationship health' indicators
- Export to PDF/Word, printable briefing packs, or email digests
- Backfill, cleanup, or migration of historical engagement data; v1 shows what exists as it exists
- Mandatory acknowledgment or approval step before a mission can be started ('must review history' gate)
- Per-record sensitivity or classification tagging and redaction beyond the existing access model
- Cross-country or regional rollup views (for example, all engagements across a region)
- Edit or annotation of engagement history from the history view; it is strictly read-only
- Notifications or alerts when new engagements are logged for a country
- Defining 'full' history to include sources outside the CRM (email, calendar, document stores)
- Point-in-time (as-of-engagement-date) snapshots of Contact name and role, and any coordinator-maintained 'last verified' field
- A dashboard, report, or review queue for the access log; the log is write-only in v1 and is consulted only during incident investigation
- Retention cutoffs or redaction of old engagement summaries (a human policy decision)

### Success Criteria
- For 100% of the ~100 member countries, the Engagement History tab loads the first page (up to 50 records) in under 3 seconds at the 95th percentile, including countries with 1,000 or more engagement records. (measures S1)
- Permission test: in acceptance testing with at least 4 user profiles, including a PM with access to only one Mission in a multi-mission country, 0 Engagement records are displayed that the user cannot view elsewhere in the CRM, and 0 Engagements from the other missions or projects appear for the single-mission PM, including Engagements reachable only via a Contact link. (measures S2, S1)
- Summary consistency test: for every test profile, the S3 engagement count and most-recent date equal the total and top row of the S1 tab for that same user, and the active-mission count includes only missions the user can access (0 discrepancies). (measures S3, S2)
- No-access test: for a user without access to a selected country, the Create Mission screen shows the no-access message and 0 counts, dates, or mission numbers in 100% of at least 5 test cases. (measures S3)
- Within 60 days of release, at least 60% of new missions created have a history-view open for the same country by the creating user within the prior 24 hours, as measured from the existing access log. (measures S3, S1)
- Summary text longer than 200 characters is truncated with an expand control in 100% of tested records, and 100% of history-tab opens in testing produce exactly one access-log entry with user, country, and timestamp. (measures S1)
- Zero new required fields are added to Engagement, Contact, Mission, Project, or Country forms, verified by comparing schema and form definitions before and after release. (measures S1, S3, S4, S5)
- Filtering by date range, type, or linked Project/Mission returns correct results in 100% of a test set of at least 20 filter combinations, and each filtered view returns within 3 seconds at p95. (measures S4)
- 100% of Engagements linked to a Contact whose existing status is inactive, departed, or blank display a visible label ('inactive', 'departed', or 'status unknown') in the history view, verified against a test set of at least 20 such records, and every Contact shows its record-last-updated date. (measures S5)
- Pre-release check: before launch, a one-time query documents which Countries the PM role can currently access and the result is recorded; no access rule is widened for this release. (measures S3, S2)

## 2. Surfaced Assumptions & Deliberation Outcomes
- **[A1] 'Full history of engagement' means all Engagement records already stored in the CRM for that country (directly or via its contacts, projects, and missions), with no retention cutoff in v1. It does not include external systems.** (Challenged: `True` -> Disposition: `REVISED_AND_ACCEPTED`)
  - *Notes:* C2 challenged the no-retention-cutoff history and exposed summary text. It ended ACCEPTED after the Proposer added 200-character truncation with expand-on-click and access logging, and left retention cutoffs out of scope.
- **[A2] Project managers already have an existing CRM role and can reach Country records and the Create Mission screen. The feature is a new read view, not a new user type.** (Challenged: `True` -> Disposition: `REVISED_AND_ACCEPTED`)
  - *Notes:* C5 challenged whether PMs can reach every Country. It ended ACCEPTED after the Proposer defined a no-access message with no counts and added a one-time pre-release check of PM Country access, with no access widened.
- **[A3] The existing Country/Mission access model is the authority on who may see which Engagement records, and any institutional sensitivity or classification of engagement content is already reflected in that model. Deciding whether any engagement types need stricter handling is a policy decision for humans, not part of this MVP.** (Challenged: `True` -> Disposition: `REVISED_AND_ACCEPTED`)
  - *Notes:* C1 challenged whether the existing permission model protects an aggregate history view. It ended ACCEPTED after S2 was revised to filter per record by Mission/Project access and the tests added a single-mission PM.
- **[A4] 'Before starting a new mission' means surfacing history at the point of mission creation (an easy, visible route), not enforcing that PMs read it.** (Challenged: `True` -> Disposition: `REVISED_AND_ACCEPTED`)
  - *Notes:* C4 challenged the Create Mission summary figures leaking hidden records or implying a dormant country. It ended ACCEPTED after the figures were computed after the permission filter, labelled 'visible to you', and covered by a consistency test.
- **[A5] Existing Engagement and Contact data is good enough for a first release: Engagements carry a date, type, and link to a Country; Contacts carry a status field. Gaps and inconsistencies in older data are accepted and shown as-is.** (Challenged: `True` -> Disposition: `REVISED_AND_ACCEPTED`)
  - *Notes:* C3 challenged stale or blank Contact data. It ended ACCEPTED after the Proposer added a 'status unknown' label, the Contact's last-updated date, and a fixed note that details are current. Point-in-time snapshots and a 'last verified' field were left out of scope.

| C1 | A3 | REVISED | 1 | `ACCEPTED` | The existing Country/Mission permission model was built for the current record-level views, not for a PM browsing years of cross-mission history on a country. A PM scoped to Mission X in Country Y who opens the Country-level history tab may see engagements from other missions or projects, including notes about sensitive ministry meetings, simply because Country access exists. The acceptance test uses 3 profiles and checks only 'visible elsewhere', which could pass while the new aggregate view still exposes content that was previously hard to reach. S1 also pulls engagements 'directly or via contacts, projects, and missions', so an Engagement linked to a Contact in a mission the PM cannot access might leak through the contact link. Required: the query must apply the per-record mission and project scope filter, not only a country-level check. The test must include a PM with access to one mission in a country and confirm other missions' engagements are absent. |
| C2 | A1 | REVISED | 1 | `ACCEPTED` | 'Full history with no retention cutoff' means a PM starting a new mission sees summary text and linked contacts from many years ago, including former ministers and staff, authored by long-departed coordinators. Old free-text summaries may hold candid assessments of individuals, or information shared under a past administration, that were never meant for wide re-reading. S1 also exposes the full summary text by default, which is the most confidential field. Required: show summary text in a truncated or expand-on-click form, and log access to the history view per user and country so a leak or misuse can be investigated. Without access logging, nobody can tell who read a country's history after an incident. |
| C3 | A5 | REVISED | 1 | `ACCEPTED` | S5 displays each Engagement's linked Contact's current name and role, as stored. Contacts turn over constantly across 100 countries. A 2019 engagement with the then Minister of Trade will show whoever now occupies that Contact record or its stale role, or a contact flagged active that has actually left. A PM may then call or email the wrong person at the ministry, or believe the relationship is still live. Only the status field is labelled, and A5 accepts that it is likely stale or blank. Required: show the Contact's name and role as of the engagement date if captured, or at least a 'last verified' date on the contact, and treat a blank or unknown status as an explicit 'status unknown' label rather than showing nothing. Otherwise the 100% label criterion only covers records already marked correctly. |
| C4 | A4 | REVISED | 1 | `ACCEPTED` | S3 shows 'count of currently active missions' and the most recent engagement date on the Create Mission screen. If this count includes missions the PM cannot access, it leaks the existence of confidential missions in a country. The summary figures may also be computed before permission filtering, so total engagements could differ from what the tab lists, confusing users and revealing hidden records. A sparse or stale 'most recent engagement' date may also mislead a PM into thinking a country is dormant when contacts simply were not logged. Required: compute all summary counts after the same permission filter as S1, and label them 'visible to you'. |
| C5 | A2 | REVISED | 1 | `ACCEPTED` | The Proposer assumes the PM role already exists and can reach every Country record. In practice PMs may hold broad roles or be assigned countries loosely, and a new mission creator will often select a country they have no prior access to, which is the very moment they want history.
