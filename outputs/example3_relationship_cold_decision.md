# Deliberation Decision Document
**Feature Request:** The CRM should alert us when our relationship with a country goes cold.

**Termination Reason:** `CONVERGENCE_ALL_ISSUES_RESOLVED_OR_ESCALATED (CRITIC_SIGNALED_CONVERGENCE)` | **Final Disagreement Score:** `0.0` | **Confidence (Proposer / Critic):** `0.72` / `0.30`

## Executive Summary
V1 runs a nightly job that marks a country cold when no Engagement has been logged for more than 90 days (one global threshold) and the country has no active Mission. Each cold country gets one in-app notification per 30-day window. The job is idempotent and self-heals after a skipped night, and alerts auto-clear within 24 hours of a new Engagement. Coordinators enter nothing. The main trade-off was simplicity against accuracy: the Critic pressed on false positives from a single threshold and uneven logging, and on false negatives from bulk-logged Engagements. These were bounded by active-mission suppression, a reason line, a day-one cap of 25%, and a one-off two-part release gate, instead of per-country settings or Engagement-type filtering. The agents agreed on the engineering scope but could not settle the sensitivity of alert content. Three scope items are pending a human decision on C4: the in-app notification content, the weekly email digest, and send-time recipient resolution with its fallback role. Humans must decide whether a country name with a 'cold' label may appear in email and whether restricted Engagements may count toward the days-since figure. They must also confirm the 3-of-10 release-hold cutoff.

## 1. Agreed Scope
### Included in Scope (v1 MVP)
- Nightly job computes last_engagement_date per Country as the most recent existing Engagement linked to any Contact, Mission, or Project of that country. The job runs with system permissions and counts restricted Engagements toward 'warm', because they can only suppress an alert and never reveal anything. It is derived on the fly with no new entity or manual field.
- A country is cold when days since last_engagement_date exceed one global threshold (default 90 days, one system config value, no per-country settings) AND the country has no active Mission. Active-mission countries are suppressed. Countries with no Engagement history are treated as cold with a 'no recorded engagement' label and counted in the day-one cap.
- Level-triggered state instead of edge-triggered: one auto-filled column, Country.last_alerted_at, written by the job. Each night a cold country with no alert in the last 30 days gets one in-app notification. Reruns are idempotent and a skipped night self-heals on the next run. Nothing is entered by coordinators.
- Alert auto-clears within 24 hours of a new Engagement being logged for the country. No acknowledge, snooze, or dismiss action is required of the coordinator.
- Nightly job failures are written to the existing platform job logging and alerting for engineering. No new screen or queue is added.
- One-off pre-release release gate, not a product feature: a single query over existing Engagement data lists unflagged countries whose most recent Engagement is a bulk or multi-recipient record. Coordinators review a sample of about 10 of them and the result is recorded. No new screen, table, field, or ongoing review step is added.

### Pending Human Decision (relies on an escalated issue)
- In-app notification carries only the country link, the days-since count, and a reason line ('No logged engagement for N days; threshold 90; no active mission'). Last engagement date and last contact are not stored in the notification. They are rendered on the Country page at view time through the existing Country/Mission/Engagement permissions. *(waits on C4)*
- Weekly email digest to each recipient lists currently cold countries with country name, days-since band, and a link into the CRM. It contains no contact names, no engagement dates, and no engagement details. *(waits on C4)*
- Recipient resolution happens at send time. Recipients are the currently assigned Regional Coordinators whose accounts are active and who still hold Country access. If none qualify, the alert goes to existing Operations-role holders with access to that country, so the country never goes silently unalerted. Which role is the right fallback is an organisational decision for humans. *(waits on C4)*

### Explicitly Excluded from Scope
- Sentiment, relationship-health, or ML scoring of engagement quality
- Per-country, per-region, or per-contact thresholds, seasonality calendars, exclusion lists, and any settings someone must maintain per country
- Weighting or filtering Engagements by type in the running product, for example mass-mailing detection. This is revisited only if the release-gate or post-launch evidence shows masking.
- Dashboards, 'cold countries' lists or queues, job-health screens, and cross-country ranking views
- Snooze, acknowledge, dismiss, approval, or escalation workflows
- Automatic ingestion of email, calendar, or phone activity to infer engagement
- New entities or tables, and new required fields for Regional Coordinators
- Alert delivery to channels outside the CRM other than the weekly digest (no SMS or chat), and to users outside existing access roles
- Per-recipient recomputation of last-engagement values based on each recipient's Engagement visibility
- Defining which countries are strategic priorities, what counts as 'cold' diplomatically, the email classification policy, and the fallback-recipient policy. These are decisions for humans, not product design.

### Success Criteria
- Every non-suppressed country over the 90-day threshold gets exactly one in-app notification per 30-day window. Verified by back-test and by rerunning the nightly job twice on the same day (idempotent, zero duplicates).
- After a simulated skipped nightly run, the next run produces all missed notifications with no manual intervention.
- Alerts auto-clear within 24 hours of a new Engagement being logged for that country.
- Countries with an active Mission produce zero cold alerts.
- Zero new fields or manual steps are required of Regional Coordinators. The only new schema is the auto-filled Country.last_alerted_at column.
- Weekly digest emails contain zero contact names and zero engagement dates (automated test on rendered output).
- Delivery test: 100% of cold countries resolve to at least one active recipient with Country access. Countries with no assigned active coordinator resolve to the fallback role.
- Day-one back-test: no more than 25% of ~100 countries are flagged, including no-history countries. If the share is higher, the threshold is escalated to humans before release.
- Release gate, precision: coordinators confirm a sample of 10 flagged countries as truly cold, and the result is recorded.
- Release gate, false negatives: coordinators review a sample of about 10 unflagged countries whose most recent Engagement is a bulk or multi-recipient record, and the result is recorded. If 3 or more of the 10 are judged genuinely cold (the 3-of-10 cutoff is a proposed default for humans to confirm), release is held and the threshold and Engagement-type question goes back to humans before launch.
- Weekly digest is delivered to 100% of recipients who have at least one cold country.

## 2. Surfaced Assumptions & Deliberation Outcomes
- **[A1] 'Cold' means no logged Engagement of any type with any contact, mission, or project of the country within a single global threshold of 90 days.** (Challenged: `True` -> Disposition: `REVISED_AND_ACCEPTED`)
  - *Notes:* C1 (single global threshold) ended ACCEPTED after active-mission suppression and a reason line were added. C5 (go-live and edge-trigger semantics) ended ACCEPTED after the move to level-triggered alerting with Country.last_alerted_at.
- **[A2] Engagement records already in the CRM are logged as part of normal work and are complete enough to act as the activity signal; Regional Coordinators will not be asked to enter anything extra.** (Challenged: `True` -> Disposition: `REVISED_AND_ACCEPTED`)
  - *Notes:* C2 ended ACCEPTED after the Proposer added a precision gate and a false-negative release gate with a 3-of-10 hold rule. In-product Engagement-type filtering stays out of scope.
- **[A3] Each country already has an assigned Regional Coordinator in the existing access model, and alerts go to that person and respect existing Country/Mission permissions.** (Challenged: `True` -> Disposition: `ESCALATED_TO_HUMAN`)
  - *Notes:* C3 (unassigned or departed coordinators) ended ACCEPTED with send-time recipient resolution and an Operations-role fallback. C4 (email confidentiality and restricted Engagements) was ESCALATED_TO_HUMAN, so the notification, digest, and recipient-resolution scope items stay pending.
- **[A4] In-app notification plus a weekly email digest is sufficient timeliness; real-time alerting is not needed because relationships go cold over weeks, not hours.** (Challenged: `False` -> Disposition: `ACCEPTED`)
  - *Notes:* Not challenged. In-app notification plus a weekly digest was accepted as sufficient timeliness.

## 3. Open Questions Requiring Human / Policy Sign-Off
- **[C4] Head of Diplomatic Security (data classification and clearance authority), with Legal; the product owner should route this:** Engineering cannot settle the policy on what an alert may reveal. The Proposer removed contact names and engagement dates from the email and kept only country name, a days-since band, and a CRM link. Two points remain. (1) May a country name with a 'cold' label appear in an email that leaves the CRM's access-controlled boundary? If not, the digest becomes link-only with no country names. (2) May restricted Engagements count toward 'warm' in the system-run job, even though the days-since figure can then reflect an Engagement the recipient cannot see? Please also confirm that the fallback recipient role, currently Operations-role holders with country access, is the right one.
  - *Why it is here:* The Critic escalated it as a policy question outside engineering's authority.

| C1 | A1 | REVISED | 1 | `ACCEPTED` | A single global 90-day threshold treats Norway and a country with one biannual ministerial cycle identically. Regional Coordinators will quickly learn these are noise and start ignoring the digest, so the one genuinely cold high-priority country gets lost in the same pile. | C2 | A2 | DEFENDED -> REVISED | 2 | `ACCEPTED` | The signal is last logged Engagement, but logging is voluntary and uneven. Turnover compounds this: when a coordinator leaves or a country is reassigned, the backlog of unlogged contact makes the countries look cold exactly when handover is weakest. Also, 'any Engagement of any type' lets low-value records (automated or mass-distribution mailings, a logged circular) reset the clock, hiding a truly cold relationship. The 'complete enough' assumption is untested; the day-one back-test only measures how many are flagged, not whether those flags are true. |

In the other direction, a former coordinator who still holds Country access could keep receiving emails with last-contact names for a country they no longer work on. | C4 | A3 | REVISED | 1 | `ESCALATED_TO_HUMAN` | The alert and the weekly email include the name of the last contact engaged.
