# Deliberation Decision Document
**Feature Request:** We need a better way to track who is the right person to contact in each country.

**Termination Reason:** `CONVERGENCE_ALL_ISSUES_RESOLVED_OR_ESCALATED (CRITIC_SIGNALED_CONVERGENCE)` | **Final Disagreement Score:** `0.0` | **Confidence (Proposer / Critic):** `0.74` / `0.45`

## Executive Summary
v1 adds a 'Who to contact' panel on each country page that recommends one primary and one backup contact per existing role type, chosen by a deterministic rule over existing data, with no new tables and no manual input. It also adds a 'No engagement logged in 180 days' badge shown next to the actual last-engaged date, a confirmed and audited 'No longer in post' action with 'Reinstate', and access filtering applied before ranking. The main trade-off argued was how far a recency-based, automatically derived pick can be trusted without implying that the person is in post or is the correct diplomatic channel. The one-line 'why' evidence, its caption and the advisory label, along with the related success criterion, are pending a human decision on C5. That decision is whether the product may designate a recommended contact by role at all or must list all active role holders.

## 1. Agreed Scope
### Included in Scope (v1 MVP)
- Country page 'Who to contact' panel. For each existing contact role type (country representative, ministry contact, project lead, mission delegate) it shows one recommended contact, chosen by a deterministic rule over existing data: status is active, linked to an active Mission or Project in that country, most recent Engagement date, with a fixed tie-break on the lowest contact ID.
- A backup contact: the second-ranked contact under the same rule, shown beneath the primary for each role type.
- A 'No engagement logged in 180 days' badge on any recommended contact with no logged Engagement in the last 180 days, shown next to the actual last-engaged date so users can judge it themselves. The threshold is a single global constant, not a per-country setting. Before release, one query on a production data snapshot measures the share of recommendations that would be badged, and the constant is adjusted globally if the badge fires on most recommendations.
- Contacts whose existing status field is inactive, departed or similar are excluded from recommendations. A 'No longer in post' action on the contact card sets that existing status after a confirmation dialog. Each status change records actor and timestamp (existing audit log if there is one, otherwise two auto-filled columns on Contact), and a 'Reinstate' action on the contact card restores the previous status. There is no approval workflow.
- The panel and any country/role filter in existing contact search return only the countries and contacts the user can already access under the existing Country/Mission permissions. The user's access filter is applied to the candidate set before ranking. The 'why' line (project names, mission names, engagement dates) and the backup contact are built only from records visible to the viewer. If a linked Project or Engagement is not visible, that element is omitted from the line. No new RBAC is added.
- The 'right person' recommendation is computed at read time by a query over Contact, Engagement, Mission and Project links. No new tables are created and nothing is manually designated.

### Pending Human Decision (relies on an escalated issue)
- A one-line 'why' under each recommendation, built from existing fields and always showing the evidence it rests on: last-engaged date, the linked Project or Mission, and the contact record's existing system-maintained 'last updated' date (no new field, nothing entered by hand). A fixed caption states: 'Ranked by recent activity; does not confirm the person is currently in post.' The recommendation is labelled advisory. *(waits on C5)*

### Explicitly Excluded from Scope
- Manual designation of a 'primary contact' per country, and any other new data-entry field for Regional Coordinators
- New tables or entities for routing rules, contact preferences or a topic taxonomy
- Per-country or per-user configuration of staleness thresholds or ranking weights
- Approval, review or verification workflows for contact changes
- Alerts, notification queues, or dashboards of stale contacts or recent status changes across countries
- ML or scoring models for contact ranking
- Encoding diplomatic protocol, precedence or the proper channel for a given topic. These are policy decisions for humans and are not product design.
- Automated verification of contact validity via email bounce, external directories or ministry feeds
- Custom RBAC or classification-based visibility rules beyond the existing Country/Mission access model
- Bulk import or cleanup tooling for historical contact data
- Ranking across role types or any single country-level 'best contact'. Each role type is recommended independently.

### Success Criteria
- For at least 90% of countries with one or more active contacts, the panel shows a recommended contact with zero manual input from Regional Coordinators and no new required fields.
- In a usability test with 5 Operations or Country Engagement staff, each finds the recommended contact for a named country and role in under 30 seconds.
- On a seeded test dataset, 100% of recommended contacts with no Engagement in over 180 days show the badge, none with a more recent Engagement do, and every badge sits next to the actual last-engaged date. A pre-release query reports the badge rate on a real data snapshot, and the constant is reviewed before release if more than 50% of recommendations are badged.
- In permission tests with users who have restricted access, 0 hidden contacts, countries, project or mission names, engagement dates or backup contacts appear in the panel, the 'why' line or filtered search. The recommendation shown to a restricted user equals the one computed over only their visible records.
- In tests, 100% of 'No longer in post' changes require confirmation and record actor and timestamp, and 'Reinstate' restores the prior status and returns the contact to the ranking.
- *Pending:* 100% of recommendations display a 'why' line, the caption that it does not confirm the person is in post, and an advisory label. 0 inactive or departed contacts are recommended in the test dataset. *(waits on C5)*

## 2. Surfaced Assumptions & Deliberation Outcomes
- **[A1] 'Right person' means the right contact by role in a given country. v1 uses the four existing contact role types as the only purpose key, not a new topic or purpose taxonomy.** (Challenged: `False` -> Disposition: `ACCEPTED`)
  - *Notes:* Not challenged; accepted as written, with the four existing role types as the only purpose key.
- **[A2] Existing data (contact status, engagement history, project affiliations, active missions) is accurate enough to derive a useful recommendation automatically. The Coordinators' own manual input is not required.** (Challenged: `True` -> Disposition: `REVISED_AND_ACCEPTED`)
  - *Notes:* C1 challenged that engagement recency does not show a person is in post; the Proposer revised the design to show evidence dates and a caption, and the Critic accepted it for an MVP.
- **[A3] A single global 180-day no-engagement threshold is an acceptable proxy for a possibly outdated contact. It is a hardcoded constant, not a per-country setting.** (Challenged: `True` -> Disposition: `REVISED_AND_ACCEPTED`)
  - *Notes:* C4 challenged the single global 180-day badge as noisy; the Proposer revised it to a factual label shown beside the last-engaged date plus a pre-release badge-rate query, and the Critic accepted.
- **[A4] Users of the feature are internal Operations and Country Engagement staff, and existing Country/Mission access controls already determine which contacts each user may see.** (Challenged: `True` -> Disposition: `REVISED_AND_ACCEPTED`)
  - *Notes:* C2 raised leakage of restricted data through the 'why' line and backup contact; the Proposer revised so access filtering happens before ranking and permission tests are broadened, and the Critic accepted.
- **[A5] The recommendation is advisory decision support. Who is the diplomatically appropriate channel for a given matter remains a human judgment, and the feature encodes no protocol or policy.** (Challenged: `True` -> Disposition: `ESCALATED_TO_HUMAN`)
  - *Notes:* C5 questioned whether a single recommended pick implies the correct channel; the Proposer defended its structural mitigations and the Critic escalated to a human as a policy question, so it remains open.
- **[A6] Contacts who leave their post are marked through the existing contact status field by staff who notice it, and no external verification feed is available in v1.** (Challenged: `True` -> Disposition: `REVISED_AND_ACCEPTED`)
  - *Notes:* C3 challenged the one-click status change; the Proposer added confirmation, actor and timestamp recording and a Reinstate action, and the Critic accepted, with no mechanism to push staff to flag departures.

## 3. Open Questions Requiring Human / Policy Sign-Off
- **[C5] Head of Diplomatic Protocol or equivalent policy owner (product owner to route):** Engineering cannot settle this policy question: may the product designate a single 'recommended' contact per role type, labelled advisory, or must it list all active role holders for a country without a recommended pick? The concern is that coordinators will treat the pick as the correct channel for every matter, including where protocol requires a specific channel such as the foreign ministry. The answer decides whether the 'why' line, caption and advisory label ship as proposed or the panel falls back to a display-only list of all active holders by role, which needs no schema change.
  - *Why it is here:* The Critic escalated it as a policy question outside engineering's authority.

| C1 | A2 | REVISED | 1 | `ACCEPTED` | The rule ranks by 'most recent Engagement date' over existing data, so a contact who was merely emailed or met most recently outranks the actual current ministry contact. After a ministerial reshuffle, the departed minister's office or an outgoing contact often has the freshest logged engagement (farewell meetings, handover calls) and is still marked active, because nobody has flipped the status. The Regional Coordinator then sees a confident 'Last engaged 12 Jan' recommendation for the wrong person and sends a sensitive message to someone who no longer holds the post. Recency of engagement is a proxy for relationship activity, not for being currently in post. The panel needs to surface what the recommendation depends on, such as when the contact's status was last confirmed, rather than presenting data of unknown accuracy as the answer. |
| C2 | A4 | REVISED | 1 | `ACCEPTED` | The panel is a new aggregation surface that pulls Contact, Engagement, Mission and Project links, and a computed 'why' line is built from engagement and project fields. Existing Country/Mission permissions may gate contact records, but not necessarily the joined data. Example: a staff member with access to Country X sees 'lead on Project Y; last engaged 12 Jan' where Project Y or the engagement note belongs to a mission they cannot access. The 'why' line leaks restricted mission or engagement metadata, and the backup contact leaks a contact who is hidden from that user. The permission test criterion only checks that contacts and countries do not appear. It does not check the 'why' text, project names, engagement dates or backup contact. The read-time query must also apply the user's access filter before ranking, not after, or the ranking changes by viewer and can silently reveal a hidden contact's existence. |
| C3 | A6 | REVISED | 1 | `ACCEPTED` | The one-click 'No longer in post' action sets the existing status, with no approval step and no audit stated. Any user with edit access can erroneously or maliciously mark the correct ministry contact as departed in a single click, instantly removing them from recommendations and promoting the backup to primary. There is no confirmation, undo or record of who changed it and when. Departures are also reported secondhand, so a mistaken click is easy. Conversely, the model depends on 'staff who notice it', and the proposal has no mechanism to make that happen at the volume of 100 countries with frequent turnover. The result is a stale-status problem and a trust problem that both feed the wrong-contact risk. Minimum fix: a confirmation step, an actor and timestamp audit trail, and a simple reversal. |
| C4 | A3 | REVISED | 1 | `ACCEPTED` | The single global 180-day badge will misfire in both directions. In low-tempo countries where engagement is legitimately once or twice a year, nearly every recommended contact shows 'may be outdated', so coordinators learn to ignore the badge. Meanwhile, a contact who left 20 days ago but was in a recent farewell meeting shows no badge. Because the badge fires for most low-engagement countries and misses the highest-risk transitions, it will quickly become noise and add false confidence. It is also unclear whether Engagement logging is consistent enough for 180 days to be meaningful. The MVP should at minimum measure the badge rate on real data before fixing the constant, and show the actual last-engaged date next to the badge so users can judge it themselves. |
| C5 | A5 | DEFENDED | 1 | `ESCALATED_TO_HUMAN` | A single top recommendation with a backup labelled 'advisory' will be treated as the answer by busy coordinators. In practice, the sensitive case is a country where protocol requires a specific channel, such as the foreign ministry rather than a project lead. The rule could pick a project lead who has the freshest engagement and promote them above the ministry contact for outreach on a political matter. The proposal rightly keeps protocol out of scope, but it does not say how the interface avoids implying that the pick is the correct channel for every matter. Whether the product may rank by role at all, or must display all active role holders without a single pick, is an institutional policy decision. |
